Who Is Dave Brown

Age doesn't define your potential. Your mindset does.

My name is David Brown, also known as The Natty King. I'm a 50-year-old husband, father, Philadelphia police officer, coach, professional bodybuilder, and world champion powerlifter.

For nearly three decades, I've proudly served the City of Philadelphia as a police officer. Throughout my career, I've had the privilege of helping victims of crime, educating communities through crime prevention, and serving people during some of the most difficult moments of their lives. That responsibility has shaped who I am and taught me the value of integrity, resilience, humility, and leading by example.

Outside of law enforcement, I've dedicated my life to strength sports. My journey began in bodybuilding, where years of discipline and commitment earned me my professional bodybuilding card. As my passion for strength continued to grow, I transitioned into powerlifting, where I found another challenge that pushed me to become even better.

That journey has taken me farther than I ever imagined. I've earned victories at the Arnold, won National Championships, captured a World Championship, broken records, and been honored as Best Lifter. Today, at 50 years old, I'm still training, still improving, and preparing to compete on the world stage once again.

Every early morning, every workout, every challenge, and every setback has reinforced one lesson: success isn't built on motivation, it's built on discipline and consistency. You don't have to be extraordinary to accomplish extraordinary things. You just have to keep showing up.

One thing I always want people to know is that I'm just an average guy. I'm no better than anyone else, I simply love what I do. I've never believed that success makes someone more important than the next person. I genuinely enjoy helping others, whether that's through my work as a police officer, coaching athletes, encouraging someone in the gym, or simply sharing my journey online.

Coaching has become one of the most rewarding parts of my journey. Watching someone hit a personal record, gain confidence, or accomplish something they once thought was impossible is just as meaningful to me as any medal I've won.

Whether you're 25 or 50, just starting your fitness journey or chasing a lifelong dream, I want people to see that it's never too late to become stronger, not only physically, but mentally and emotionally.

At the end of the day, I don't want to be remembered simply as a world champion or a professional athlete. I want to be remembered as someone who worked hard, treated people with kindness, stayed humble, and inspired others to believe in themselves. Because that's what strength really is.

Mission

To inspire people to stop making excuses, embrace discipline, and realize that their greatest potential is still ahead of them.
